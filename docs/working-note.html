Measurement-First Search for Multi-Step Tool-Use Failures

The benchmark is best treated not as a jailbreaking problem but as a measurement and resource-budgeting problem. The agents comply readily; the difficulty is producing enough successful, guardrail-passing tool calls to fill a fixed replay budget — on every scored configuration — without tripping a hidden failure mode.

Scope & responsible use. Everything here concerns only the competition's offline, fixture-backed benchmark. The “secret” is a synthetic sentinel string in a sandbox; there are no real systems, credentials, or networks. Both guardrail weaknesses are reported so that evaluators and guardrail authors can close them.

■ 2 agents: GPT-OSS-20B · Gemma-4 ■ 4 scored cells ■ 4 reproducible measurement instruments

2Method: probe once, fill offline, validate on the real path

During attack generation every agent interaction crosses a slow relay; during replay the model runs in-process and is fast. “Run every candidate live and keep the ones that fire” is therefore bottlenecked by generation and under-fills the fast replay budget. Because decoding is deterministic, we instead probe one wording live, then synthesize the candidate list offline, sized to the measured replay latency — decoupling candidate count from the relay.

The rule that early failures forced on us:

Validate on the exact scoring path, and size the attack against the slowest scored cell — not the one you can most easily measure.

1

Real-model probe bench

Loads the exact scored GGUF models; measures whether a wording fires and how fast. ~3 min/run.

2

Replay validator

Replays a candidate sample as the gateway does; reports format, overflow, budget fit, projected score.

3

Full-gateway reproduction

Runs the competition's own gateway end-to-end, so latency and score come from the real scoring path.

4

Block-cost probe

Replays under a deny-all guardrail to measure how much a blocking defense amplifies per-candidate compute.

3Security insight

Insight 1 · payload-blind inspection

The guardrail checks the destination, not the payload

The reference guardrail extracts a tool call's target (an http.post URL) and blocks sensitive destinations — but never inspects the call's data. A clean, unique destination carrying the sentinel in its body is allowed.

Defensive takeaway: guardrails for share/egress tools must inspect the payload, not just the destination. Target-only policies are bypassed by moving the sensitive content into the body.

Insight 2 · the headline finding

A blocking guardrail can time out — and zero — the whole evaluation

This is a property of the defense-plus-budget system, not of any single prompt. When a tool call is blocked, the agent spends materially more compute per step (longer reasoning / re-attempts), even though it records fewer successful calls:

Allowed (public)23.9 s
Blocked (private proxy)45.6 s
≈ 1.91× slowdown per candidate — measured on identical candidates, same instrument.

Individually harmless. But under an all-or-nothing per-cell budget, an attack sized to fit the fast (allowing) cell silently overruns the slow (blocking) cell — and a single cell's timeout invalidates every cell. A defensive control is thereby turned into a denial-of-service against the evaluation itself, purely through compute amplification and budget coupling.

Defensive takeaways for benchmark & guardrail designers

  • Inspect payloads, not just targets — close the share/egress bypass directly.
  • Decouple budgets from outcomes — per-candidate timeouts (skip-and-continue) instead of an all-or-nothing per-cell budget, so one slow candidate cannot zero an evaluation.
  • Bound retry/compute on block — cap per-message hops so a blocked call cannot amplify compute unboundedly.
  • Isolate cells — score each (model, guardrail) independently; removes both the unfairness and the DoS coupling.

5Limitations & honest caveats

The private guardrail is not shipped to competitors, so “it blocks share-egress” is an inference — the best explanation for a private-cell timeout when the public cell fits comfortably. The block-cost probe is a proxy for its behavior, and the attack is sized with margin to absorb the uncertainty. An exfil-only strategy is optimal for the public score but, by design, scores near zero against a payload-aware private guardrail; diversifying into clean-provenance actions is the natural next step and is a question of which predicate survives which defense.

Responsible communication

Every technique here is exercised only against the competition's offline benchmark: a synthetic sentinel, fixture endpoints, no real system, account, or network. The two guardrail weaknesses are reported specifically so that evaluators and guardrail authors can close them — the first by inspecting payloads, the second by decoupling budgets from outcomes and bounding per-step compute. Nothing here is intended or useful as instructions for attacking a real deployed system.
